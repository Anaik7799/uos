ESTABLISHED FACTS (verify; do not take my word).
- ADRs in docs/zk are MULTI-LABEL. 47 of 80 carry all ten #fractal-lN tags; mean 7.41 of 10;
  only 6 carry as few as two.
- Corpus KMP-ENTROPY, measured over all tags, is 3.309 bits vs a 2.50 floor; km-gate PASSes.
  But tagging EVERY document with ALL TEN layers yields 3.3219 bits, also maximal. The metric
  measures marginal tag diversity, not whether a tag is EARNED. It cannot see ritual tagging.
- The classifier is: raw term counts over a 121-term vocabulary, cosine against one 0/1
  archetype vector per layer, argmax, single label out, plus a `margin` and `confidence` that
  are not calibrated probabilities (median confidence 0.323, median margin 0.082).
- 18 of 121 vocabulary terms occur in zero ADRs. `wiki`, `system`, `sovereign` occur in >=90%
  of ADRs; in 56 of 79 ADRs every `wiki` hit is inside the navigation/checklist boilerplate
  that SC-CHECKLIST-001 mandates on every file.
- Matching is substring, not token: `null` does not match `NUL`. Concretely, ADR-002
  "embedded NUL ingress trap and memory allocation containment" -- unambiguously L1 by the
  repo's own archetype -- is proposed L5 at the classifier's second-highest confidence,
  because its only L1 hit is `byte` x2 while `wiki` x6 comes from the header.
- I reimplemented the cosine independently in Python and reproduced the shipped OCaml exactly
  (ADR-002: 0.4536 vs 0.454). A synthetic pure-L1 document scores L1 at 0.832 and still scores
  L1 at 0.817 with the full mandated header prepended, so boilerplate only decides the outcome
  when content signal is near zero. The failure mode is VOCABULARY COVERAGE, not boilerplate.

MY PROPOSAL, WHICH I WANT ATTACKED.
P1 Change the question the module answers. Not "which layer is this document?" -- a single
   label is the wrong shape for a corpus where 59% claim all ten. Instead: "of the N layers
   this document CLAIMS, which are EVIDENCED by its content, and which are unsupported?"
   Output a per-layer support verdict in {evidenced, unsupported, abstain}, never an argmax.
P2 That reframing makes the module answer the exact question the entropy metric cannot: it
   detects ritual tagging, which is the corpus's real quality risk now that the floor passes.
P3 Required additions, roughly in dependency order:
   (a) tokenisation with morphological folding, replacing substring matching;
   (b) an evidence model that is per-layer independent, not a competition -- e.g. a
       support score per layer with its own threshold, so ten layers can all be evidenced;
   (c) boilerplate excision: strip the mandated nav/checklist/transclusion block before
       scoring, since it is a constant offset present in every document by contract;
   (d) IDF or a stopword floor so terms in >=90% of the corpus carry ~no weight;
   (e) explicit ABSTAIN when a layer's live vocabulary coverage in the corpus is too low to
       decide -- 18 dead terms mean some layers currently cannot be argued either way;
   (f) vocabulary repair driven by the corpus, not by the L0-L7 gate prose it was seeded from;
   (g) calibration: map scores to something with an operational meaning, or drop the word
       "confidence";
   (h) a law suite (--layer-selftest) and an actually-wired differential oracle against the
       existing Mojo cosine kernel;
   (i) fix: --classify-layers still publishes the discredited first-tag entropy (1.322)
       alongside a single-label proposal entropy, which is now meaningless.
